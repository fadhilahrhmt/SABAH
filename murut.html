The Hill People

The Murut are one of the major indigenous ethnic groups in Sabah. Traditionally, the Murut people lived in the inland and mountainous areas of Sabah, especially around Keningau, Tenom, Pensiangan, and Nabawan. The word “Murut” means “hill people” or “people of the interior.” The Murut community is known for its rich cultural heritage, traditional longhouses, colorful clothing, beadwork, music, and warrior traditions. In the past, they were skilled hunters and farmers who depended on nature for their daily lives.

Log_02 // Hill People

The Lifestyle

"Traditionally, the Murut people lived in longhouses built using bamboo and wood. A longhouse could accommodate several families living together as a community. Farming, especially hill paddy cultivation, was an important part of their lifestyle. The Murut people also used bamboo in many aspects of life, including cooking, crafting tools, musical instruments, and building houses.”

[ MENS_ATTIRE ]

The traditional attire for Murut men is a powerful reflection of their warrior heritage and deep connection to the forest, defined by garments crafted from natural materials and intricate symbolic details. The ensemble features the Babaru Puputul, a sleeveless jacket made from processed tapa bark, paired with the Aba Puputul, a matching bark loincloth that serves as the traditional lower garment. To complete the silhouette, men wear the Tupi Sinulatan, a distinctive headgear adorned with the majestic feathers of the Argus pheasant or hornbill, which historically signified the wearer's bravery and social status within the tribe.

[ WOMENS_ATTIRE ]

for the Murut women, the traditional attire is specifically known as the Pinongkolo, consisting of a sleeveless black cotton blouse and a long wrap-around skirt. These garments are meticulously embellished with tiny, colorful beads arranged in ancient geometric motifs like the tina-uh (bamboo shoots) and linangkit (embroidery), which represent forest spirits and ancestral folklore. The silhouette is anchored by the Pipirot, a heavy ceremonial belt made from rows of silver coins and strands of red, yellow, and white beads that signify wealth and status. To finish the ensemble, women wear the Sinikot, a set of three elaborate hairpins often linked by silver chains that secure the hair in a traditional bun and symbolize the beauty of the Murut spirit.
